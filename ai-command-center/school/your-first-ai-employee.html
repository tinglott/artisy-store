Your First AI Employee — what one actually is (and is not)

The problem this lesson solves: everybody tells you to “use AI,” and hands you a chatbot. A chatbot answers questions. That is not an employee. An AI employee is a system with a job: defined tasks, your information to work from, tools it is allowed to touch, hard limits it may never cross, and a human — you — who checks its work. This lesson teaches the process behind a real one, using ours.

1 · Meet the employee

Our first AI employee is Aria, the receptionist for ARTISY. Her job: greet customers, answer questions about our products from our own verified information, point people to the right page, and take messages. She is not a general chatbot — ask her something outside her job and she says so instead of inventing an answer. She was examined before she was trusted: 13 practical work tests, a red-team test where we tried to trick her into making things up, and a knowledge test — she passed all of them, and her graduation was signed by a human. She works in preview mode today, supervised, while we finish proving her in live conditions. Every lesson in this school will tell you the true status of what you are looking at, the same way.

2 · The advanced process, step by step

An AI employee is built and run like this — this is the process, in order:

  1. Write the job first. One page: the tasks, the tone, and the things this employee must never do. If you cannot write the job, you are not ready for the employee.
  2. Give it a brain with sources. The employee answers from your documents — your prices, your pages, your policies — not from the open internet's imagination. Grounded answers or no answers.
  3. Give it ears and a voice. Customers reach it the way they reach you: your website, your phone line, your messages. One employee, every door.
  4. Give it memory. It remembers your business and its past conversations, so customers are not starting over every time — and it forgets what you tell it to forget.
  5. Give it tools, with limits. It may look up an order. It may book a call. It may not move money, change prices, or promise things in your name. Tools are granted one at a time, like keys to rooms in your building.
  6. Train and examine it. Practice tasks, trick questions, edge cases. In our Academy an employee must pass scored trials — including producing zero invented facts — before it is allowed near a real customer.
  7. Supervise forever. A human reviews, spot-checks at the destination (not just the report), and can pull any key back at any time. Automation without supervision is how businesses embarrass themselves.

3 · The guardrails

  • Never invent. If the information is not in its sources, the employee says it does not know and offers a human path. A confident wrong answer is the one unforgivable error.
  • Never touch money. AI employees track, draft, and prepare. Humans approve, send, and pay. Always.
  • Never act beyond the job. A receptionist does not give medical, legal, or financial advice — in any business, including ours (and our founder is a nurse who still will not let an AI practice nursing).
  • Ask before anything goes public. Posts, prices, promises, and messages in your name wait for your yes.

5 · Try it this week — your checklist

  1. Pick one repetitive job in your business that eats your time (answering the same questions is the classic first job).
  2. Write its one-page job description: tasks, tone, and three “never” rules.
  3. Gather the source documents that job needs — your real prices, hours, policies — in one folder.
  4. Write ten questions customers actually ask, with the correct answers. Those become your employee's first exam.
  5. Decide, in writing, what this employee will never be allowed to touch. Money and public promises are the usual first two.

That is one honest hour of work, and it puts you ahead of most businesses “using AI” today — because you now have a job, sources, an exam, and limits. The rest of this school teaches you to build each piece.
