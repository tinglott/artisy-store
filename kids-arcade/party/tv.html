Kids Games — four events, one combined score

Pick Kids Games (4 events) above, then press Start. You play four different events back to back (a different four every day, same for everyone), 30 seconds each, and your scores add up into one combined score with its own medal: Bronze 120, Silver 240, Gold 400. Three hearts per event, and you can restart instantly any time. Dance Party can be one of the four events.

How to play

Phone: tap Enable motion, tilt to aim, flick your wrist to act. Computer: move the mouse to aim, click or press Space to act. TV: use AirPlay screen mirroring (iPhone) or Cast screen (Android/Chromecast), then play with the phone in your hands.

Fishing: line up the boat, flick to drop the hook, fish are always released. Pad boxing: aim at the glowing pad (left, middle, right) and punch it, no contact with anyone. Bubble blaster: splash bubbles with a water burst.

Ninja slice: flick to slash fruit, skip the rocks. Archery: aim at the moving target, flick to release (closer to the middle scores more). Hurdle sprint: flick or click to jump each hurdle, a clean jump scores 10.

Safari Photo Hunt: animals wander the savanna — line up the viewfinder and tap to snap a photo. No shooting, just photos. Target Splash Range: tap the moving ducks and ring buoys to splash them. Rhythm Gloves: punch the glowing pad ON the beat — the music is made by the game itself, a simple original drum-and-bass pattern, never a known song. Paper Plane Javelin: aim and tap to throw — higher aim flies further, and the wind pushes the plane. Table Tennis Rally: slide the paddle with your mouse or tilt and keep the rally going. Shuriken Dodge: tap the foam toy stars to deflect them, or tilt out of their path.

Dance Party: pick a song and a difficulty, press Start, then tap the arrow pads (or arrow keys / WASD) when the falling arrows hit the line — Perfect within 150 ms, Great 220 ms, Good 300 ms. Make space and stay safe before you dance. Your TalkingPhotos dance clip plays on the song screen once Ting picks the dancer.

Kids Games: four events back to back (a different four every day), 30 seconds each — scores add up into one combined score, with its own medal (Bronze 120 / Silver 240 / Gold 400).

Hearts: you get 3 hearts each round. A mistake costs one — when they run out the round ends, and you can restart instantly with one tap. No waiting, no payment, ever.

Combos & power-ups: hits in a row build a combo multiplier (up to ×5). Five hits in a row with no miss unlocks that game's power-up for a short time — earned by skill only, never random, never bought. Levels: every game has 3 levels inside the round — score 40 for Level 2, 100 for Level 3. Faster play, more points.

What is popular right now

VR boxing

The Thrill of the Fight 2 (realistic simulator, Quest 3 and PC early access), Creed: Rise to Glory Championship Edition (cinematic, Quest, PC, PSVR2), and FitXR (rhythm fitness) lead 2026 lists. Creed has a training mode and the Thrill of the Fight original is still a top Quest seller.

Mike Tyson note: I did not find a current Mike Tyson VR title. His name is tied to Nintendo’s classic Punch-Out, which is owned and licensed, so it can’t be copied or used in a kids product. This series uses original characters instead.

VR fishing

Real VR Fishing is the default pick: over one million sales (Jan 2026), cross-play on Quest, Steam, Pico and Galaxy XR, up to four anglers, and a seventh-anniversary event in Sept 2026. Bait! is a free, cartoonish first VR game; Fishing in VR is a calm seated boat experience.

VR hunting and shooting

Virtual Hunter (Korpi Games, May 27 2026, $24.99, Quest and PSVR2) is the newest serious hunting sim. Others include Green Hell VR and Legendary Hunter VR. Super Hot VR and Blade & Sorcery: Nomad sit in the Quest top sellers. These are realistic or violent, so for kids the series swaps them for water and bubble blasters.

Ninja and Olympic-style games

Ninja and slicing

Fruit Ninja VR 2 (Halfbrick, Quest and PC VR) adds blades, bows and mini-games to the classic slicer, and has a no-bomb Zen mode. Ninja Blade: Sword Master (early access June 2026, Quest) has you deflect incoming shurikens. Fruit Ninja VR: Ghostbusters (May 2026) and Sword Master VR are similar. Kid-safe takeaway: slice fruit, deflect, dodge. No blood, no people targets.

Olympic and sports variety

Sports Scramble mixes tennis, baseball, bowling and archery with silly twists (a racket can turn into a golf club). Eleven Table Tennis is the realistic ping pong leader, Walkabout Mini Golf the mini golf pick, and Summer Games VR offers archery and javelin. QuiVr is a fantasy archery game. Variety ideas: archery, hurdles, javelin, table tennis, mini golf, bowling, long jump.

Phone plus TV: proven pattern

Rolocule’s Motion Tennis and Motion Tennis Cast already turned a phone into a Wii-style racket using AirPlay, Chromecast or Miracast mirroring. Open source examples: Significant-Hobbies/motion (body-pose controller mirrored to TV) and Open Game System (phone as controller, Cast Kit to TV). AirConsole runs phone-as-controller games on any TV browser.

What parents actually want (research, Nov 9 2026)

Parents filter first on: no ads, no purchases, no accounts, no data collection, no chat, works offline. Short rounds with natural stopping points, instant restarts, visible progress (high scores, levels, medals), variety every session, fair difficulty that scales, and calm sound. Full notes: RESEARCH.md in the rebuild folder.

Three things the research changed in this rebuild: (1) lives are in-round retries only — a lost round restarts instantly, never a wait timer or payment; (2) the daily challenge has no streak, no penalty for missing a day, no notifications — it is one shared puzzle per date; (3) power-ups are earned by skill only (a perfect streak), never random, never bought, no virtual currency, no slot-machine visuals.

Dance Party — DDR-style dancing (built Nov 10, 2026)

Mode 13: a canvas-drawn dancer reacts to your play — she cheers on Perfect, wobbles on a Miss, and strikes a pose on a full combo. Four arrows scroll to the line; tap the big on-screen pads or use arrow keys / WASD. Timing is kid-friendly on purpose: Perfect within 150 ms, Great 220 ms, Good 300 ms — about 4.5 times kinder than arcade DDR. Fifteen of Ting's own original songs, each with a measured BPM and three difficulties (Easy — arrows on the beat, Medium — off-beats, Hard — doubles and jumps). The music is made by the game itself — an original WebAudio beat in each song's style at its measured tempo, never a known song. No camera, ever — the game judges arrow taps, not bodies. TalkingPhotos dance clips play on the song-select screen and as a celebration reward once Ting picks the dancer character; the clips are placeholders until then.

Production path

Prototype in the browser (this page), then port to Godot 4 with phone sensors over WebRTC or WebSocket to a TV browser page. Ship through Payhip or ARTISY as a web link, then Google Play and App Store. Add parent-lock, no ads, no chat, a break reminder every 10 minutes, and a clear-space warning before motion play.

Kid safety

Wrist strap, clear play area, adult nearby, short sessions. No realistic weapons, no animal harm, no real boxer likeness. No ads, no purchases, no accounts, no chat, no data collection — everything is saved on the device only.

Time for a stretch!

You have been playing for 10 minutes. Stand up, drink some water, wiggle your arms — then come back for more.

Clear some space first

Motion play means waving your phone around. Make sure you have clear space around you, nothing breakable nearby, and an adult close by. A wrist strap helps!
