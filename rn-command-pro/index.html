Hospice — Painting the Picture

Hospice visit documentation built around one principle: paint the picture of the terminal patient. Every note documents the terminal diagnosis, pain, appetite, ADLs, appearance, sleep, cognition, dyspnea, edema, and education — with measurable observations (weights, mid-arm circumference, % of meals eaten, scale scores) and a comparison to the last visit, so decline is visible on paper. Includes the standardized scales hospice runs on — PPS, Karnofsky, FAST, and NYHA — and HOPE visit types.

Words that paint decline

Use precise descriptors — anorexic, cachectic, dyspneic at rest / with minimal exertion, frail — and follow each with “as evidenced by” plus what you actually saw. Avoid: stable, no change, eating well, no issues, no problems noted — those describe an absence of decline and can undermine eligibility.

HOPE (CMS, since Oct 1, 2025)

HOPE replaced the HIS for hospice quality reporting. Records: Admission, up to two HOPE Update Visits in the first 30 days, a Symptom Follow-Up Visit when symptom impact is moderate or severe, and Discharge. Submitted through iQIES. This form’s visit types match those records.

Recertification & the custodial trap

Recertification documentation needs measurable support data: decline indicators plus scale scores compared to admission and the last recert. Needing continued services is custodial — it is not, by itself, proof the patient is still terminal. If two notes disagree, explain the inconsistency in the note where it appears.
